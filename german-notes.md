# German Notes

A consolidated summary of all notes in this repo. Some comparisons are to
Afrikaans, since that is the reference language used throughout.

---

## Pronunciation: every letter

Sounds are given as the nearest English equivalent. German is highly phonetic —
letters almost always sound the same way, unlike English.

### Vowels

| Letter | English equivalent | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | "ah" as in *father* (short: *cut*) | **a**lles, W**a**sser |
| **e** | "eh" as in *bed*; at the end of a word, the "uh" in *sofa* | g**e**ht, Tass**e** |
| **i** | "ih" as in *sit*; long, "ee" as in *see* | **i**st, w**i**r |
| **o** | "oh" as in *note*, lips rounded | w**o**, Br**o**t |
| **u** | "oo" as in *boot* (never "yoo") | d**u**, g**u**t |
| **y** | like **ü** (see below); rare | T**y**p |

### Umlauts

| Letter | English equivalent | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **ä** | "eh" as in *bed* / the *a* in *care* | K**ä**se (cheese) |
| **ö** | no English match: say "eh" as in *her* with lips rounded for "oh" | h**ö**ren, zw**ö**lf |
| **ü** | no English match: say "ee" as in *see* with lips pursed for "oo" | f**ü**nf, gr**ü**n |

### Consonants

| Letter | English equivalent | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **b** | "b" as in *bed*; at the end of a word, "p" | **B**lume, hal**b** ("halp") |
| **c** | rare alone; "ts" before e/i, otherwise "k" | **C**afé |
| **d** | "d" as in *dog*; at the end of a word, "t" | **d**u, un**d** ("unt") |
| **f** | "f" as in *fish* | **F**arbe |
| **g** | hard "g" as in *go*; at the end of a word, "k" | **g**ut, Ta**g** ("tahk") |
| **h** | "h" as in *hat*; **silent after a vowel**, just lengthening it | **H**and vs. ge**h**en ("gay-en") |
| **j** | "y" as in *yes* | **j**a ("yah") |
| **k** | "k" as in *king* | **k**alt |
| **l** | "l" as in *light* (always light, never the dark *ball* l) | **l**angsam |
| **m** | "m" as in *man* | **m**ein |
| **n** | "n" as in *no* | **n**ein |
| **p** | "p" as in *pen* | **P**apier |
| **q** | always **qu** = "kv" | **Qu**alität ("kva-") |
| **r** | throaty, from the back of the throat (like Afrikaans/French *r*); at the end of a word, a soft "uh" | **r**ot, Vate**r** ("fah-tuh") |
| **s** | **"z"** as in *zoo* before a vowel; "s" as in *sun* elsewhere | **s**ehr ("zair"), i**s**t |
| **t** | "t" as in *top* | **T**isch |
| **v** | **"f"** as in *fish* | **v**ier ("feer") |
| **w** | **"v"** as in *van* | **w**o ("voh") |
| **x** | "ks" as in *box* | Ta**x**i |
| **z** | **"ts"** as in *cats* | **z**wei ("tsvai") |
| **ß** | "ss" — sharp s, never a "b" | hei**ß**e, Stra**ß**e |

### Letter combinations

| Combination | English equivalent | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **au** | "ow" as in *how* | Fr**au**, **au**ch |
| **ei / ai** | "eye" as in *my* | m**ei**n ("mine"), **Ei** |
| **ie** | "ee" as in *see* (opposite of **ei**) | v**ie**r, L**ie**be |
| **eu / äu** | "oy" as in *boy* (the *oi* in Afrikaans *mooi*) | D**eu**tsch, B**äu**me |
| **ch** (after a, o, u, au) | throaty, as in Afrikaans *g* / Scottish *loch* | a**ch**t, Bu**ch** |
| **ch** (after e, i, ä, ö, ü) | soft, like the *h* in *huge* | i**ch**, spre**ch**en |
| **sch** | "sh" as in *shoe* | **sch**warz, Ti**sch** |
| **sp / st** (at word start) | "shp" / "sht" | **sp**rechen, **St**raße |
| **th** | just "t" — never the English *th* | **Th**eater |
| **ck** | "k" | Glü**ck** |
| **ng** | "ng" as in *singer* (no hard g) | ju**ng** |
| **tsch** | "ch" as in *church* | Deu**tsch** |

### Alphabet names (for spelling out loud)

Ask with: **Buchstabieren Sie, bitte** = Spell it, please.

A "ah" · B "beh" · C "tseh" · D "deh" · E "eh" · F "eff" · G "geh" · H "hah" ·
I "ee" · J "yot" · K "kah" · L "ell" · M "em" · N "en" · O "oh" · P "peh" ·
Q "koo" · R "err" · S "ess" · T "teh" · U "oo" · V "fau" · W "veh" · X "iks" ·
Y "üpsilon" · Z "tsett" · ß "ess-tsett"

So: **VW** = "fau-veh", **BMW** = "beh-em-veh".

## Formal vs. informal

This distinction runs through everything:

- **du** = informal "you" (friends, family, your own age) → verb ends in **-st**
- **Sie** = formal "you" (strangers, teachers, officials) → verb ends in **-en**
- **ich** (I) → verb ends in **-e**

| ich (-e) | du (-st) | Sie (-en) |
|---|---|---|
| ich wohne | du wohnst | Sie wohnen |
| ich komme | du kommst | Sie kommen |
| ich heiße | du heißt | Sie heißen |

Pairs to know:

- Wie heißt du? / Wie heißen Sie? — What is your name?
- Wie geht's dir? / Wie geht es Ihnen? — How are you?
- Wo wohnst du? / Wo wohnen Sie? — Where do you live?
- Woher kommst du? / Woher kommen Sie? — Where are you from?
- Wie alt bist du? / Wie alt sind Sie? — How old are you?

Titles: **Herr** = Mr, **Frau** = Ms/Mrs.

Pronouns: **Sie** (u/you formal) · **dich** (jou/you) · **euch** (julle/you all) ·
**deine** (your) · **meine** (my).

## Introducing yourself

- Ich heiße Christopher. / Ich bin Chris. / Mein Name ist Christopher Klopper.
  (**Mein** Name — not *meine*, because *Name* is masculine.)
- Mein Vorname ist … (first name) · Mein Nachname / Familienname ist … (surname)
- Ich wohne in Kapstadt. — I live in Cape Town.
- Ich komme aus Kapstadt, aus Südafrika. — I come from Cape Town, from South Africa.
- Ich bin Ausländer. — I am a foreigner. (*Ausland* = abroad; think "out-land".)
- Ich spreche ein bisschen Deutsch. — I speak a little German.
- Ich lerne Deutsch. — I am learning German.
- Ich verstehe nicht, was Sie sagen. — I don't understand what you are saying.
- Ich habe eine Frage. — I have a question.
- Wie sagt man "fridge"? — How do you say "fridge"?
- Wie heißt das auf Deutsch? — What is that called in German?

*"Ich bin ein Berliner"* — the famous JFK line, jokingly "I am a jam doughnut".

## Nouns and articles

**All nouns are capitalised**, always. Every noun has one of three articles:

- **der** = masculine — der Kaffee, der Tee, der Tisch (table), der Kühlschrank
  (fridge), der Ring, der Ohrring, der Himmel (sky)
- **die** = feminine — die Tasche (bag), die Tasse (cup), die Tafel (board),
  die Blume (flower), die Sonne (sun), die Karotte, die Münze (coin),
  die Nacht, die Straße
- **das** = neuter — das Auto, das Handy (mobile phone), das Wasser

Plurals take **die**: die Bäume (the trees).

## Numbers (Zahlen)

null 0 · eins 1 · zwei 2 · drei 3 · vier 4 · fünf 5 · sechs 6 ("zeks") ·
sieben 7 · acht 8 · neun 9 · zehn 10 · elf 11 · zwölf 12

Above twenty, units come first: **zweiundzwanzig** = 22 (literally "two-and-twenty").

Useful: Wie ist deine Telefonnummer? / Wie ist Ihre Handynummer?

## Colours (Farben)

- Mein Auto ist **silber**.
- Die Blume ist **rot**.
- Das Wasser ist **blau**.
- Die Sonne ist **gelb**.
- Die Bäume sind **grün**.
- Die Orange / Die Apfelsine ist **orange**.
- Die Nacht ist **schwarz**.
- Der Kühlschrank ist **weiß**.
- Der Ring ist **gold**.
- Der Himmel ist **grau**.

Was ist Ihre Lieblingsfarbe? — What is your favourite colour?
(*Lieblings-* = favourite.) — Ich mag Silber. (I like silver.)

## Negation: nicht vs. kein

- **nicht** negates verbs and adjectives: Ich weiß **nicht**.
- **kein** negates nouns: Das ist ein Auto → Das ist **kein** Auto.

---

## Sound shortcuts from Afrikaans

- Afrikaans **g** → German **ch** (achtung, Buch)
- Afrikaans **oi** in *mooi* → German **eu** (Deutsch)
- **aua** = "ow!" (the cry of pain)
- German **s** before a vowel sounds like English **z**
- **zurück** — the *ruck* part is the Afrikaans/English "rug"/back
